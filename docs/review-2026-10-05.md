# Response to the implementation review of 5 October 2026

The review ("SBCI implementation review", 5 October 2026, of v0.0.1.dev0 at
commit 4b1850b) built the wheel, ran the suite and then tested the
mathematics rather than the agreement with MATLAB. It reported eleven
findings and three documentation points. This note says what was done about
each; PORTING.md item 8 has the causes and the measured sizes, and the
commit that answers the review carries the code, the tests and the documents.

**Every finding was real.** Each was reproduced with independent probes
before anything was changed. Three of them -- the Legendre derivative
recurrence, the normalization order of the transported square-root density,
and the projection objective -- are errors in the MATLAB references that the
port had reproduced to rounding, which is exactly what the agreement tests
measured and could not see past. The package now computes the right thing
by default and keeps the reference arithmetic behind `reference=True`
(ENCORE and `project`) and `strict_upstream=True` (ConSEAL), so that the
comparisons with the references still run; the documents say which
agreement figures hold only under those flags.

## The documentation points

- *ConSEAL paper equivalence.* The settings `delta=0.1, step_clamp=inf,
  viscosity=0, threshold=1e-6` are now described as the unregularized public
  update, not the paper's: the fork also composes the vertex map directly and
  differentiates the kernel its own way, which the package does not
  reproduce (USAGE.md, PORTING.md item 7).
- *Migration to MSMAll data.* USAGE.md qualifies the example: a group warp is
  a template-coordinate operation; a subject's MSMAll maps also went through
  that subject's own registration, which has to be composed first
  (`SphereMap.from_gifti(native_sphere, msmall_sphere)`).
- *Verification language.* README.md no longer calls the methods "verified
  ports"; VERIFICATION.md opens its list of things to challenge with
  "agreement with MATLAB is not correctness", names the three shared errors,
  and lists the independent invariants the suite now asserts.

## What was re-verified after the corrections

- The 22 MATLAB-reference tests and the ConSEAL comparison (Tier 4), run on
  the cluster with the reference flags: 22 passed, including the basis
  Laplacian against the reference dump (which confirms the reference's
  divergence is the wrong one), and the ConSEAL stages agree with the MATLAB
  run as before -- the six-iteration cost trace within 3.3e-7 relative at
  every iteration, the template to 2.0e-5.
- The heavy methods on two released subjects, recorded in both arithmetics
  on one CPU type: the reference arithmetic reproduces the recording of
  2 October bit for bit (seven arrays, worst difference 0.0), and the
  corrections move the results modestly -- ENCORE reaches costs lower by
  4e-4 to 1.1e-3 relative, its warped vertices land 0.04 degrees (0.25 at
  most) from where the reference arithmetic put them and the aligned density
  differs by 1.5% of its largest entry; ConSEAL's aligned endpoints move
  0.02 degrees (0.11 at most); the default projection returns the fitted
  scores to 0.9% where the reference's objective ran 2.2% lower still
  (PORTING.md item 8).
- The alignment figures, regenerated with the corrected divergence: the
  known-warp recovery comes out the same (ENCORE 1.63 to 0.20 degrees, in
  eleven steps rather than ten; ConSEAL to 0.11; the migrated warp 0.33
  degrees from the true one).
- The ConSEAL gradient measurement of PORTING.md item 7, rerun: the port's
  gradient now points 11 degrees from the exact gradient of the discrete
  cost (cosine 0.981, from 0.931 with the inherited divergence), the
  reference's still 47 (0.678).

## The second list

A second reviewer's list arrived the same day, built on this review and run
against the corrected code: nine findings and a dozen smaller ones. Each was
checked with an independent probe or against the code before anything
changed; PORTING.md item 9 has the verdicts, causes and sizes.

- **Confirmed and fixed:** ConSEAL's Karcher median collapsing onto the
  subject it starts from (a rounding defect the reference shares: float32
  endpoint weights put that subject's squared norm just below 1, and the
  reference's coincidence snap misses it -- the "median settles on
  sub-212116" the documents explained by geometry); `local_test(terms=)`
  testing the intercept when a covariate is constant; zeros next to the
  diagonal of the FC exchange file; sidecar names cut at the first dot; the
  medial wall kept as two regions in three PALS atlases; sphere plots
  showing the wrong side; ENCORE refusing every step on a raw array with a
  nonzero diagonal; warps that forgot their grid rotation once saved; and
  the smaller items -- ENCORE's identity Jacobian of 0.9965 (0.7% of the
  aligned density's mass, the reference's), the parcellation's diagonal and
  empty regions,
  bandwidth, eigenvalue-order and vertex-id checks, `save()` suffixes, a
  download that could not resume, `validate` passing files `load` refuses,
  the save paths, the `strict_upstream` docstrings and the Schaefer region
  counts.
- **Confirmed, for a different reason than given:** the ConSEAL pole
  vertices. The vertex at the north pole did follow only half of a shift,
  but a grid rotated off the poles does nearly the same; the cause is the
  velocity smoothing, which averaged frame components as scalars across a
  pole (the reference does too). It now smooths vectors in space, and the
  pole vertex follows 3.45 of 4 degrees while the endpoints around it land
  0.035 degrees from the truth.
- **Confirmed, and fixed on a closer look:** large `StationaryWarp.rotate`
  rotations were up to 1.6 degrees off at 150 degrees (2.3 at a half turn),
  because the reference holds a rotation as its own velocity field and the
  scaling-and-squaring flow realizes it inexactly. Measuring it also showed
  every later smoothing eroding the stored rotation, 3.8 of 150 degrees over
  a registration's 100 steps with today's smoothing; handed the exact
  rotation, a registration ended 0.44 degrees from the truth on average. The rotation is now held
  exactly, outside the velocity field, and the same registration ends at
  0.001; warps without one are unchanged, bit for bit.

## The third list

A third list, on 6 October, re-ran the first two against the corrected code
and audited what the corrections had changed. Each item was checked first
(PORTING.md item 10); all hold, one in part.

- **The ConSEAL pole vertices, only partly fixed before.** At a vertex
  exactly on the coordinate axis the tangent basis zeroed the m = 1 sine
  fields and their curl partners, 30 of 510, through a `sin(theta)` clamp
  the reference has too; the second list's smoothing had hidden it. The
  basis now takes the limit there. With `viscosity=0` the pole vertex moves
  with its neighbours (3.54 degrees among 2.66 to 3.86), where the
  reference's basis leaves it behind (2.07 among 2.22 to 3.75).
- **Problems the corrections had introduced, all fixed:** `local_test`
  testing another column when a design carries its own intercept (a
  constant column is now refused, naming both remedies; designs built with
  `add_constant` need `add_intercept=False`); coupling on atlases with empty
  regions, where a region with no SC stopped being dropped; a ConSEAL
  subject with no streamlines, or an ENCORE density with mass only on its
  diagonal, turning everything NaN (now refused by index); the ConSEAL
  template holding the cohort twice; the FC export's diagonal of 1 reaching
  the medial wall; and a grid with a vertex just off the axis passing
  `align()` (now refused within the 1e-3 `rotate_off_poles` clears).
- **The smoothing, partly right:** it shrank rotation fields twice as fast
  as the reference about the coordinate axis, but more slowly about a
  tilted one, where the reference's frame-dependent rate is fastest. The
  cause was a curvature term in smoothing projected rather than transported
  vectors; ConSEAL now smooths with the connection Laplacian, the same
  97.6% of a rotation field left after 100 smoothings about any axis, and
  closer to the reference near the equator than before.
- **The small ones:** the CIFTI export checks the name and the metadata
  before the 16.9 GB resample, `fetch_cohort` takes a single modality as a
  string, missing family labels are refused rather than made one family,
  the HDF5 reader splits endpoints at the file's own vertex count, and the
  PALS atlas and 0.7% wordings are corrected.

## After the third list

- **Finding 8 had been closed only for an intercept column.** Dummy codes for
  every level of a factor, with `add_intercept=False`, absorb a shift as an
  intercept column does, but the untestable rule still judged them on the
  uncentred scale, so a shift of 1e6 turned every component NaN. The rule
  now asks whether the reduced model can reproduce a constant, by the rank
  the degrees of freedom are counted with, and the dummy-coded design matches
  the reference-level coding to rounding, shifted or not. Its default
  `terms` would test the implied intercept with the rest, so it is refused,
  the message naming `terms=`. PORTING.md item 8 has the record.

## Still open

- Whether the functional PCA should minimize the area-weighted approximation
  error (finding 6) is a method decision for WP1; the current objective is
  the reference's and is now described as such.
- The fit records each component's scores one alternating step before its
  final vector, as the reference does, so on the training cohort `project()`
  agrees with `Reduction.scores` to the fit's tolerance (1e-3 at the default
  `tol_outer`) rather than to rounding unless the fit is run to convergence.
- The paper's own ConSEAL update (direct vertex-map composition, its
  derivative machinery) is not implemented; the package ports the public code.
